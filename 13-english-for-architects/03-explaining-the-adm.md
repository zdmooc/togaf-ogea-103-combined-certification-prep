# Explaining TOGAF and the ADM in English

## 1. Réponse courte : What is TOGAF?

> TOGAF is an enterprise architecture framework. Its core method, the ADM, helps structure architecture work from initial context and vision through domain architectures, migration planning, implementation governance, and architecture change management.

## 2. Réponse plus professionnelle

> I use TOGAF as a framework rather than as a rigid process. The ADM gives a common structure for understanding business drivers, stakeholders, baseline and target architectures, gaps, transformation options, migration planning, governance, and change. The method must be tailored to the organization and can be iterative.

## 3. Preliminary Phase

> The Preliminary Phase establishes the architecture capability: governance, roles, principles, repository, method, and tailoring. It answers the question: how will the organization perform and govern architecture work?

## 4. Phase A

> Phase A establishes the Architecture Vision and the scope of the engagement. We clarify the business drivers, stakeholders, concerns, value, constraints, risks, and high-level target direction.

Key distinction:

> Preliminary prepares the architecture capability; Phase A starts a specific architecture engagement.

## 5. Phase B

> Phase B develops the Business Architecture. We focus on business capabilities, services, processes, organization, and the business changes required to reach the target state.

## 6. Phase C

### Data

> The Data Architecture defines how information is structured, owned, governed, exchanged, and managed across its lifecycle.

### Application

> The Application Architecture defines the application services, interactions, responsibilities, and portfolio changes required to support the business target.

Key distinction:

> Data Architecture focuses on information; Application Architecture focuses on the application services that use and manage that information.

## 7. Phase D

> Phase D defines the Technology Architecture: the platforms, technology services, infrastructure patterns, runtime capabilities, and standards required to support the target business, data, and application architectures.

## 8. Phase E

> Phase E translates architecture gaps into implementation-oriented work packages and transition architectures. It identifies how the target can be realized through a coherent transformation roadmap.

## 9. Phase F

> Phase F prioritizes and sequences the work packages. It considers value, cost, risk, dependencies, and organizational readiness to create the Implementation and Migration Plan.

Key distinction:

> Phase E structures the transformation; Phase F determines the migration order and priorities.

## 10. Phase G

> Phase G governs implementation. We check that delivery remains aligned with the approved architecture through architecture contracts, compliance reviews, traceable decisions, and controlled exceptions.

## 11. Phase H

> Phase H manages architecture change. We monitor new business drivers, regulations, technologies, and other changes, assess their impact, and decide whether a local change or a new ADM cycle is required.

## 12. Requirements Management

> Requirements Management is continuous across the ADM. Requirements are identified, analyzed, traced, validated, updated, and linked to architecture decisions throughout the lifecycle.

## 13. Explain the full ADM in 45 seconds

> The ADM starts by establishing the architecture capability and framing the architecture engagement. It then develops the Business, Data, Application, and Technology Architectures by comparing baseline and target states and identifying gaps. Those gaps are consolidated into work packages and transition architectures, prioritized into a migration plan, governed during implementation, and continuously reassessed as the business environment changes. Requirements Management supports the entire cycle.

## 14. Common interview questions

### Is TOGAF waterfall?

> No. The ADM is iterative and tailorable. It provides structure and governance, but the depth, sequencing, artifacts, and level of iteration should fit the organization and delivery model.

### Does TOGAF prescribe technologies?

> No. TOGAF structures architecture work and decision-making. Technology choices must be evaluated against requirements, principles, constraints, and the target architecture.

### What is the difference between an Architecture Roadmap and an Implementation and Migration Plan?

> The Architecture Roadmap describes the transformation trajectory and major work packages and transitions. The Implementation and Migration Plan is more detailed and prioritizes and sequences execution.

### Why are stakeholders important?

> Because architecture is not only a technical design. Different stakeholders have different concerns, and the architecture must provide appropriate views and decisions that address those concerns.
